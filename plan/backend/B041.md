## Goal
Assign a strictly increasing per-session `seq` to every sequenced frame, deduplicate retries, process acks, rate-limit senders, and keep the hot replay buffer, giving at-least-once ordered delivery.

## In scope
- Sequence stage (order 40): atomically (1) dedupe by `(sid, from, id)` for 24 h, (2) assign `seq` (starts at 1, gapless), (3) stamp `from`, `ts`, (4) append to the hot buffer.
- Redis-backed `SeqStore` (one Lua script: dedupe check, INCR, XADD to `relay:ses:{sid}:buf`, SET dedupe key) with an in-memory implementation for tests.
- Hot buffer retention: at least the last 5 000 frames or 10 min, whichever is larger, hard cap 20 000 frames; trim on append.
- Echo of a sender's own sequenced frame back to the sender with its `seq` (the ack of its send); duplicate submissions return the original `seq` without a new broadcast.
- Inbound ack handling (`ack` field and `t:'ack'`): track per-connection `acked_seq`, monotonic; reject ack > head.
- Per-member rate limit for sequenced frames: 30/s sustained, burst 100 (token bucket); excess frames dropped with `sys.slow_down` (`p.for_ms` 1000), close 4429 after 5 s of continuous violation.
- Port `DurableAppend` called asynchronously after buffer append (default no-op).

## Out of scope
- Replay on resume (B042), durable history storage (B055), fan-out delivery (B044), outbound backpressure (B046).
- Presence/ephemeral frames, which are never sequenced (B047).
- Authorisation of who may send which kind (B043).

## Acceptance criteria
1. 1 000 frames from 5 concurrent senders in one session receive seq 1..1000 exactly once each, no gaps, no duplicates, and every connection observes them in increasing order.
2. A client re-sending a frame with the same `(from,id)` after a reconnect gets the original `seq` echoed, nothing is broadcast twice, and the buffer contains the frame once.
3. The same `id` from two different members is treated as two frames.
4. The sender's echo carries `seq` and `ts` set by the server; a client-supplied `seq` is overwritten.
5. 31 sequenced frames within 1 s from one member (burst 100 already spent) yields `sys.slow_down` and drops the excess; sustained 30/s with burst up to 100 passes without drops; 5 s of continuous excess closes 4429.
6. After 30 000 appends the buffer holds >= 5 000 and <= 20 000 frames and `oldest()` = head - size + 1; frames younger than 10 min are retained while count <= 20 000.
7. `ack` greater than head returns `sys.error` `invalid_frame` and does not move `acked_seq`; `acked_seq` never decreases.
8. If `DurableAppend` rejects, the frame is still delivered and a retry with backoff (up to 5, jitter) is scheduled; the metric `relay_durable_append_failed_total` increments.
9. Assign p95 < 2 ms against Redis on the test container at 1 000 frames/s.

## Guardrails (MUST / MUST NOT)
- A `seq` MUST be assigned and the frame appended to the hot buffer atomically before any fan-out, so every delivered frame is replayable; never deliver then store.
- Seq numbers MUST be gapless per session and never reused, even if the durable append or fan-out fails.
- Dedupe MUST key on `(sid, from, id)` where `from` is the server-stamped member id, never a client value.
- The relay MUST NOT inspect `ct` here; stored frames keep `ct`, `sig` and `p` byte-identical to what the sender sent (only server fields added).
- The hot buffer is bounded (count and age); it MUST NOT grow without limit.
- Rate-limited frames MUST be dropped, not queued, so a flooding member cannot grow memory.

## Failure modes
- Redis unavailable -> sequenced frames are rejected with `sys.error` (503-class) and `sys.slow_down`; no local-only sequencing (it would fork the order).
- Lua script times out -> error to sender, no partial state (script is atomic).
- Redis restart loses the buffer -> `head` is recovered from the durable log by B042's hydration; until then sequencing is paused (503-class).
