## Goal
Throttle high-rate ephemeral signals: keep only the latest `presence.cursor` per member (max 10/s, fan-out every 100 ms) and automatically clear typing indicators after 5 s without refresh.

## In scope
- Cursor stage for `t:'presence'`, `k:'presence.cursor'` (encrypted `ct` only): per-member latest-wins slot, inbound cap 10/s, fan-out tick every 100 ms, only changed slots sent.
- Under backpressure (B046) or load, older cursors are dropped; at most 1 cursor frame per member is ever queued per connection.
- Typing auto-clear: when a member's `presence.update` has `activity:'typing'` and no refresh arrives within 5 s, the relay emits a `presence.update` for that member with `activity:'idle'` (same `status`), via the B047 service.
- Size cap for cursor frames: `ct` <= 4 KiB (cursor payloads are tiny); larger are dropped with `invalid_frame`.
- Metrics: cursors received/forwarded/dropped.

## Out of scope
- Decrypting or reading cursor content (never).
- Presence snapshot and online/offline derivation (B047).
- Client-side expiry of stale cursors after 10 s (client behaviour).

## Build against (no counterpart needed)
The mock client simulator from B011 (scripted fake clients speaking the WS protocol), the in-memory B009 KV/pub-sub implementations, and the frame fixtures in contracts/fixtures/events/; no real client is needed. Fake clock; cursor frames use random-byte `ct.c` because the relay must not interpret them.

## Acceptance criteria
1. A member sending 50 cursor frames in 1 s: at most 10 are accepted and the other members receive at most 10 frames for that member in that second, the last accepted one always being the latest value.
2. Two members each sending steadily produce at most 1 outbound cursor frame per member per 100 ms tick to each peer.
3. A cursor frame with `ct` of 4097 bytes is dropped and answered with `sys.error` `invalid_frame`; the connection stays open.
4. A typing indicator not refreshed for 5 s (+-0.1 s) causes exactly one `presence.update` with `activity:'idle'`; a refresh within 5 s resets the timer; leaving the session clears the timer.
5. Cursor frames are never sequenced or stored; `head` is unchanged after 1 000 cursors.
6. Under a stalled consumer (B046 above soft watermark), cursor frames for that connection are dropped and counted; nothing else changes.
7. The relay holds at most one pending cursor per member (memory stays flat over 1 000 000 offered frames).

- cursors.tick.test.ts: 100 ms fan-out, changed-only.

- cursors.not-sequenced.test.ts: seq/buffer untouched.

## Guardrails (MUST / MUST NOT)
- `ct` of cursor frames MUST be treated as opaque; the relay MUST NOT inspect, log or store its content or length distribution beyond the size cap check.
- Cursor frames MUST NOT be sequenced, replayed or persisted (CT-WS-PRESENCE).
- Only the latest cursor per member may be retained; there MUST be no queue of cursors.
- A member MUST NOT be able to emit presence on behalf of another (server stamps `from`).
- Typing clear frames MUST be generated by the relay from its own timer, not triggered by other clients.

## Failure modes
- Timer drift or event-loop lag -> typing clear may be late but never early; one coalesced tick processes all due members.
- Member disconnects while typing -> timer cancelled; B047's offline handling applies.
- Cursor flood from a malicious client -> excess silently dropped, repeated floods (> 10x limit for 10 s) close 4429 via B046 hooks.
## Notes
CONTRACT GAP: the cursor `ct` size cap of 4 KiB is a lane decision (contract gives only the global 192 KiB limit); product may adjust via config. Client behaviour 'cursors disappear after 10 s' is not enforced by the relay.
